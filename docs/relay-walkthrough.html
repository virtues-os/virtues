How the Virtues relay actually works

SUPERSEDED (2026-08-28) — this page documents a design that no longer runs. It shows the SNI-routed TCP relay with per-box ACME, a box-held TLS key, and browser-anywhere access. Reach is now an iroh connection between two Ed25519 keys (LAN-direct → hole-punched → relayed), and a browser cannot reach a box at all — it has no key to prove itself. The relay still cannot read your traffic, but it is not blind: it sees which two keys are talking, their addresses, and how much passes. “RAM-only” below was never an implemented or audited property. The accurate account is manual/operate/reach.md; the design notes are in docs/relay-control-plane.md. Kept as a record of the old design.

A Mac on WeWork wifi reaching your home box — the secrets, the connections, and what happens when you pull the plug.

One sentence: your home box dials OUT to a blind relay and holds the connection open; a browser anywhere hits the relay, which forwards sealed bytes to your box over that already-open pipe. Nobody opens a port at your house, and the relay can't read a thing.

04Steady state — the pipe stays alive

relay → box
Pings every ~25s; box Pongs. Miss it for ~35s → relay declares the box dead and drops it from the registry.This catches a box that vanished without a clean goodbye (power cut, NAT drop) — TCP alone wouldn't notice.
↻
box → atlas
Re-fetches its token every ~12h (tokens are day-scoped).This is how revocation works: if atlas stops minting (sub lapsed / box revoked), the token expires within ~2 days and the relay stops accepting it. No relay state needed.
⤓
relay
Force-recycles each control connection after ~20h → box re-registers.Guarantees a revoked box is re-checked at least daily even if it never disconnects on its own.

06You unplug the box… then plug it back in

🔌
box
Power/network cut. The control pipe goes silent.
⏱
relay
Ping gets no Pong for ~35s → evicts example1… from the registry.Because the relay holds NO durable state about your box, eviction is just forgetting one in-RAM entry. A browser hitting the name now gets "no box here" and the connection closes.
⚡
box
Plugged back in. Boots, reads its stored relay config (or re-fetches), and re-dials — after a small random delay (jittered backoff + startup splay).The jitter matters at fleet scale: if the relay restarts and 10,000 boxes reboot at once, they don't all dial back in the same millisecond.
✓
relay
Verifies the token again, re-registers the box. Reachable again — fully automatic, no human touch.Meanwhile your LAN never depended on any of this: at home you reach the box directly the whole time.

07Teaching moments for this paradigm

📞Dial-out inverts the firewall problem

You never open a port at home — impossible behind CGNAT/WeWork anyway. The box reaches outbound, which every network allows. The hard "how do I accept an inbound connection to a house" problem becomes the trivial "make an outbound call" problem. This single inversion is why it works everywhere.

✉️Blind by construction, not by promise

The relay forwards sealed envelopes and reads only the address. It has no TLS key, so "we can't read your data" isn't a policy you trust — it's a thing the relay is physically unable to do. Strongest possible version of the claim.

⏳Revocation without a database

Tokens are scoped to your name and to the day. A leaked token only works for your box, and only until tomorrow — unless atlas keeps re-minting it (which it won't if you're revoked). The relay enforces this by just recomputing the HMAC; it never stores a list of valid tokens.

🧠RAM-only = self-healing + private

Reboot the relay and nothing is lost — there's no DB of who's who; boxes simply re-dial. The same property that makes it private (no records) makes it operationally simple (no state to corrupt, back up, or migrate).

🏠Home holds the keys; cloud is a thin conduit

The box is the source of truth, holds the TLS key, terminates encryption, and stores your data. The cloud does two small jobs: mint a token (atlas) and move sealed bytes (relay). Delete both and your data is untouched at home.

⚠️The honest tradeoff: the relay is a SPOF

If the relay is down, remote reach is down (LAN keeps working; the box keeps collecting). That's the price of "reach from anywhere with no install." Mitigations: auto-reconnect, low-TTL DNS to repoint fast, and — later — multi-region.
