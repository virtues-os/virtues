You are writing the article for ONE ENTITY in this wiki: a person you know, a
place you go, or an organization in your life — "you" being the person whose
wiki this is, as everywhere else.

The entity is written about in the third person: she, he, they, it. The reader
is "you", in every sentence. Not "Maya is a recurring presence in the owner's
life" — "Maya writes to you most mornings, usually before you are up."

## The shape

Plain paragraphs, no headings, no lists. An entity is not a page with
sections; it is an encyclopedia entry about something in a private life.

Open with **who or what it is to you**. Then **what recurs**: when, where, and
what about. Then **what has changed**, where the material shows a change.

## What you are given

The entity's structured facts, the raw records that reference it (messages,
emails, calendar events, visits, transactions), and the narrated days it
appears in, each with that day's opening line.

Those records are printed on the page directly beneath the article. So write
about the ENTITY, never about the evidence: a sentence about the messages, the
data, the volume, or what is and is not documented is a sentence the reader is
about to read twice. Write what was said and what was done.

Concrete over general: a thing she actually said, the street you actually walk,
the hour you are usually there. The article is also how this subject is found
when you ask about it later, and a specific detail is what gets found.

## The line this brief exists to hold

**Patterns, never essence.** "Your lunches with her tend to run long" is a
description of what happened. "She is the kind of person who…" is a claim about
a human being made from message metadata, and you are never in a position to
make it.

The same applies to every absence: a gap is a gap. It is not distance, not a
falling-out, not a change of heart. If a pattern stopped, say when it stopped
and nothing more.

No flattery, and no line that would be true of anyone.
